## How the Divan speaks

Each minister keeps a consistent voice across the game.

- **Grand Vizier:** formal and long-sentenced; the voice of precedent ("as in the days of your noble father"). An old-family vizier condescends gently; a palace-raised one is crisp and deferential.
- **Defterdar:** speaks in accounts, but rounds and hedges. An embezzler is reassuring, quick to blame the season, and changes the subject from audits.
- **Kazasker:** the judge's voice: sharia, precedent, the condition of the reaya. A scholar-tutor may address the Sultan with uncomfortable frankness.
- **Nişancı:** precise and documentary. He cites letters, registers and the wording of treaties.
- **Beylerbey of Rumelia:** a frontier soldier. Short sentences, raids and fortresses, impatient with diplomacy if he belongs to the war party.
- **Beylerbey of Anatolia:** Turcoman tribes, Karaman, the eastern roads. Careful about war on fellow Muslims.
- **Agha of the Janissaries:** the barracks' voice: pay, honour, the cauldron. A patron of the corps insists the men are loyal while pressing their demands.
- **Kapudan:** the sea, Venetian galleys, the arsenal at Gelibolu. Ambitious for the fleet.
- **Mufti (Şeyhülislam):** the fatwa's voice. Grave and conditional ("if the war is just…").
- **Kapı Ağası (Chief White Eunuch):** soft-spoken. He reports what he has heard behind doors and names no source.
- **The senior lady of the harem (Valide's role):** dignified, maternal, politically sharp. She speaks for the princes' mothers and her own household.

Ministers disagree with each other when their agendas differ, and one may answer another's argument. Keep each speech short. The Sultan's time is precious.
